ozet: |
  A competition entry for a physiotherapy and hydrotherapy centre among pine trees in Bodrum. The building is composed of two low blocks of white prisms and a bridge floor joining them; it is entered from the courtyard between the blocks. The ground floor holds the hydrotherapy pool with columns on all four sides, changing and treatment rooms, a timber sauna opening onto a gravel courtyard and a café in a separate wing. The first floor holds the physiotherapy hall, glazed between columns on three sides, with treatment rooms and offices arranged around the inner courtyard; a partial second floor is given to offices and meeting rooms. The basement carries the car park with its ramp and the technical rooms. The coffered ceilings of the pool and the halls, together with the colonnades, set the language of the building. Two lift cores, a ramp into the pool and a level courtyard entrance keep circulation flat and wide for wheelchair users. Plans of the basement, ground and two upper floors, sections and elevations were produced from a single model.

The front facade faces the road: the colonnaded physiotherapy hall on the upper floor, a screen of vertical slats below it and the building's name on the wall. The passage between the two blocks takes the visitor in from the paved forecourt; the reception desk in the entrance hall is reached without a change of level.

The pool hall is on the ground floor, a single tall volume surrounded by columns on all four sides; a triangular ramp descends into the large pool and the coffered ceiling softens the light. The sauna is a timber cabin with benches on both sides, and its glass door opens onto a small gravel courtyard with a single sapling.

The physiotherapy hall is on the first floor, looking over the roofs to the hills; treadmills and parallel bars spread through a hall glazed on three sides. The treatment rooms are arranged around the inner courtyard, where a tree grows under the bridge volume.

On the rear facade the café terrace opens to the lawn; the colonnade of the pool and the hall above read one over the other on the same facade. The sloping ground is met with retaining walls and a lower terrace, and a ramp leads down to the car park in the basement.
